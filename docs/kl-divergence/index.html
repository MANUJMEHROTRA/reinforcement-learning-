Two KLs, two different answers

A true posterior has two plausible explanations. You approximate it with one Gaussian. Change which KL you minimize and watch the approximation choose between covering both modes and committing to one.

Forward KL averages over posterior samples. Missing posterior mass is expensive, so q tends to spread across both modes.

Forward KL: mode covering

KL(p || q) = ∫ p(z) log[p(z) / q(z)] dz

Samples come from the true posterior p. If p is large somewhere but q is tiny, that region contributes a large penalty. A single Gaussian often widens to cover both posterior modes, even if it puts extra mass in the valley between them.

Reverse KL: mode seeking

KL(q || p) = ∫ q(z) log[q(z) / p(z)] dz

Samples come from q. Regions q rarely visits contribute little, even if p has another mode there. The approximation can settle on one plausible mode and ignore the other. This is common in variational inference and in some RL objectives.

What “true posterior” means

p(z | x) = p(x | z) p(z) / p(x)

It is the distribution over hidden explanations z after seeing data x. In real problems it is often intractable. Here we define a small two-mode posterior exactly so you can compare an approximation q(z) against it directly.
