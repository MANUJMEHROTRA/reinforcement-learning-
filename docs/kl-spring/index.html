The Gaussian-mean case is exact: KL is a perfect parabola everywhere. For other families the parabola holds only near θ₀. Further out, higher-order terms make the bowl lopsided, much like a spring stretched past the range where Hooke’s law holds. That is also where KL’s asymmetry first shows up.

KL divergence has no time in it. The motion appears once θ is given inertia, which is what momentum optimizers do. Plain gradient descent is the heavily overdamped limit, so it slides to the bottom without overshooting.
